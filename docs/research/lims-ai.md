# How LIMS, ELN and CDS vendors build AI into their products

Research for ticket #41 (map #1). It feeds [Decide the assistant layer](https://github.com/zhipengzhu1-dotcom/09-28-2026-LIMS/issues/43). Checked 2026-09-30. Sources are vendor docs, release notes and help pages where they could be reached. Otherwise they are vendor blogs, brochures and press. **CLAIM** marks marketing or press wording that no document backs up.

## Answer

1. **No GxP LIMS vendor documents a generative-AI feature in its validated product.** LabWare, STARLIMS and Thermo Fisher SampleManager ship classical machine learning: anomaly flags, trend analytics and outcome prediction. None of them publishes a chat panel, SOP Q&A, drafting, a model name or a hosting statement for its LIMS. LabVantage launched an agent layer, CORTEX, in March 2026. Its named agents are still labelled "in development".
2. **The ELN vendors are ahead, and Benchling documents the most.** Benchling AI (GA 7 Oct 2025) has a side-panel chat, in-context buttons, a data-entry agent and agent modes. It names its model hosts and training terms. It also labels each AI write in the audit log. Dotmatics requires approval for every agent write.
3. **No chromatography vendor puts AI into its validated quantitation path.** Waters ships no AI in Empower, MassLynx or TargetLynx. Its only ML (anomaly detection) sits in a read-only cloud viewer outside Empower. Its QUAN Review "Exception Focused Review" is rules the lab sets, not AI. Shimadzu sells a locked ML peak integrator. Agilent sells a cloud model that keeps learning from each user. SCIEX OS 5.0 ships an LLM help chat, restricted to research-use-only products.
4. **Nobody has published a real GxP defence for LLM features.** No vendor states a position on the draft EU Annex 22. None publishes a validation package or intended-use statement for an LLM feature. The defences on offer are three: bullets ("GxP-ready", "Part 11 compliance"), human-in-the-loop wording, and audit-log labelling. The draft Annex 22 says generative AI and LLMs "should not be used in critical GMP applications" ([draft Annex 22 §1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)). So the vendors' silence is itself the finding: they keep LLMs out of the GMP record, or they don't say.
5. **For this LIMS:** copy draft-then-accept, attribution to the person with an AI label, answers that cite records, per-role switches, and keeping AI off Results and verdicts. Avoid standing autonomous write modes, learning models, predicted results, undisclosed hosting, and compliance-by-bullet. The full lists are at the end.

## The yardstick: draft EU GMP Annex 22

The draft was published for consultation on 7 July 2025 and closed on 7 October 2025. Its scope is "static models" with "deterministic output" in "critical applications with direct impact on patient safety, product quality or data integrity". It then says:

> "the document does not apply to Generative AI and Large Language Models (LLM), and such models should not be used in critical GMP applications. If used in non-critical GMP applications … personnel with adequate qualification and training should always be responsible for ensuring that the outputs from such models are suitable for the intended use, i.e. a human-in-the-loop (HITL)" (§1)

Dynamic models "which continuously and automatically learn and adapt performance during use" are likewise excluded from critical use (§1). Where a model gives "an input to a decision made by a human operator", the intended use must state the operator's responsibility, and the operator's performance is monitored "like any other manual process" (§3.3). Critical-use models must log a confidence score and may return "undecided" (§9) ([draft](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/5f38a92d-bb8e-4264-8898-ea076e926db6_en?filename=mp_vol4_chap4_annex22_consultation_guideline_en.pdf)).

EMA held a workshop on 30 June–1 July 2026 to "help shape a risk-based approach to the use of generative artificial intelligence (AI) in medicines manufacturing", seeking input on "control and mitigation measures such as guardrails" ([EMA event page](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/events/good-manufacturing-practice-multistakeholder-workshop-expert-contributions-artificial-intelligence-guidance-development-annex-22)). The draft text is unchanged, so the LLM exclusion still stands. What an assistant may do under these rules is ticket #39's question. This document only measures vendors against them.

### Benchling (Benchling AI)

- **What the AI does.** Launched 7 Oct 2025 ([blog](https://www.benchling.com/blog/introducing-benchling-ai)). As of August 2026, one Chat absorbs the former Ask, Deep Research, Compose and Analysis agents. Alongside it sit SQL Writer, Notebook Check ("flags entry typos, inconsistencies, incomplete sections"), Plate Annotator, the Data Entry Agent and a Model Hub ([help: Getting started](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/25260337033741-Getting-started-with-Benchling-AI)). The Data Entry Agent turns PDF and Excel files into result or registration tables. It runs repeated cross-model passes and shows whether results were "verified across repeated runs". The user then clicks "Approve and insert rows" or "Acknowledge verification failure and insert rows". Source files are attached to the entry, and the page lists "Risks from AI mistakes" ([help: Data Entry Agent](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/34900709662989)).
- **Where in the UI.** Both surfaces exist. Chat is a side panel opened from a sparkle icon, and it answers with "citations and chip links" to records. It supports Agent Skills and MCP connectors ([help: Chat](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/45766024411405-Use-Benchling-AI-chat)). In-context sparkles sit in table headers and an "AI tools" menu in the entry toolbar.
- **Agent modes.** Every chat runs in "Default" or "Full actions" mode. Default mode "does not allow an agent to make changes directly to Benchling objects". It can "Create draft entries and canvases - you choose whether to save them", and can "Self-improve by writing skills or creating new agents". Full actions mode "lets an agent take any action you're personally permitted to take", such as editing entries, registering entities, transfers and creating plates. There, "most actions happen without further approval". Only "admin-level actions, such as creating or editing schemas" always prompt, and that approval "only covers the current plan". Admins switch Full actions on or off "for individual users" in the Tenant Admin Console ([help: Agent Modes and Approvals, updated 9 Sep 2026](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/48328875643533-Agent-Modes-and-Approvals)).
- **Model and hosting.** Models run on Amazon Bedrock (Claude, Llama, Nova), Google Vertex AI (Gemini, Claude) and OpenAI. Providers may hold data only "temporarily or in-memory" and are "prohibited from training on customer data". Prompts and outputs are stored in Benchling's own tenant-isolated database. AI acts with the user's permissions. Scientific models run in Benchling's AWS under change control. An optional toggle, off by default, sends prompts and outputs to an observability service that keeps them 30 days. No region statement was found ([help: AI security, updated 31 Jul 2026](https://help.benchling.com/hc/en-us/articles/20901323667853); [privacy and security](https://www.benchling.com/ai/privacy-and-security)). Admins switch each capability on per tenant or for "select users", and can set tenant-wide "custom guidelines".
- **GxP claims.** The Chat article says AI writes are "attributed to the user who initiated the AI action… with an additional label indicating the action was performed by Benchling AI". The Agent Modes article says "All actions an agent takes are captured in Benchling's audit log and version history". There, edits show as "separate Clients" in the audit log and as "edited via Benchling AI" in version history. The security article says any write "requires the user to accept the output or action". **That conflicts with Full actions mode.** The marketing page lists "GxP / 21 CFR Part 11 compliance" and "Audit trail and citation architecture for AI outputs" (CLAIM). No statement puts Benchling AI inside the Validated Cloud's validated scope. There is no Annex 11, Annex 22 or CSA statement for AI.

## Patterns worth copying

1. **AI output is a draft that a named person accepts.** Benchling's default agent mode creates drafts that "you choose whether to save". Its Data Entry Agent waits for "Approve and insert rows". Dotmatics approves every write. In this LIMS, an assistant proposal would sit beside the record, like the pencil drafts from the UI prototype (#23). It becomes a Record Version only when the signed-in person saves it. It never carries an Electronic Signature or a Signature Meaning of its own.
2. **Attribute the change to the person, and label it as AI.** Benchling records the initiating user plus "performed by Benchling AI" in the Audit Trail and version history. The Audit Trail entry here could carry the person, their role and Lab, and an assistant marker with the model version and prompt reference.
3. **Answers cite the records they came from.** Benchling Chat answers with "citations and chip links". An explain-and-find answer about a Deviation or a Run should link each claim to the record it came from, so the reader checks the source, not the prose.
4. **The assistant has exactly the signed-in person's access.** Benchling AI "acts with the user's permissions". Here that means the same lab-scoped seam as every other read. A Customer User's assistant sees only their Customer's work.
5. **Switch it on per feature and per role, and audit the switch.** Benchling toggles each capability per tenant or user. SCIEX adds an "Access AI Assistant" permission, though its notes list no audit event for the assistant. A per-role Admin setting fits here. Changing it should itself be audit-trailed, and so should each use of the assistant.
6. **Keep AI off the path that computes Results and verdicts.** Waters puts its ML in read-only analytics outside Empower. SCIEX limits its LLM to RUO use and help text. The draft Annex 22 bars LLMs from critical GMP use. This already matches standing rules: "nothing shows a pass it didn't compute", verdicts come from a Calculation Version, and changes to Critical Data need a second person. An assistant may point out a problem. It never sets a value, a Run Check outcome, an Integration Declaration or a verdict.
7. **Deterministic exception review, named honestly.** Waters' Exception Focused Review uses lab-set rules and does not call itself AI. This LIMS's Run Checks, Import cross-checks and Review Checklist evidence are the same idea. They should be presented as rules, and kept apart from anything probabilistic.
8. **A named, pinned model endpoint.** SCIEX appears to let the customer configure the endpoint. Benchling names its hosts and no-training terms. This LIMS should name the model, its version and the provider's retention terms in the validation record. Hosting and cost belong to #42.
9. **If ML ever touches chromatography, lock it and keep the analyst's check.** Shimadzu's model "cannot learn from the user's peak data", and analysts still check every result visually. For this Lab, integration stays in MassLynx anyway (#20).

## Patterns to avoid

1. **Standing autonomous write modes.** Benchling's "Full actions" mode, where "most actions happen without further approval", is one. So is LabVantage's "executing intelligent sample routing" (CLAIM), and SampleManager ATR's review "without user intervention". Each lets AI change the record without a person accepting that change. That clashes with Annex 22 §1's human-in-the-loop for LLMs and with this map's "act is never" proposal.
2. **Docs that contradict the product.** Benchling's security article says every write "requires the user to accept", while Full actions skips acceptance. A GxP customer's validation leans on those words. This LIMS's intended-use statement must match what the code allows.
3. **Models that keep learning in use.** Agilent's cloud model is trained continuously from users' manual integrations. The draft Annex 22 says dynamic models "should not be used in critical GMP applications".
4. **Predicting results instead of measuring them.** SampleManager Profiling estimates "the outcome of unexecuted tests" and can "fail early samples that would not pass". A predicted outcome is not a Result. Showing one breaks the rule that the system never shows a verdict it did not compute from a measurement.
5. **Compliance by bullet point.** "GxP-ready controls" (LabVantage), a "GxP / 21 CFR Part 11 compliance" bullet (Benchling) and Zifo's Part 11 citation all say nothing about the AI's intended use, validation or limits. None states whether the AI is inside the validated scope. This LIMS should write down, per assistant tier, its intended use, whether it is critical, who is responsible for the output, and how that is tested.
6. **Undisclosed model, hosting or data flow.** No model or hosting is disclosed for LabVantage CORTEX, Dotmatics Luma, Zifo or STARLIMS Analytics. The Empower Data Viewer uploads results to the vendor's cloud. As an open Part 11 system (#10), this LIMS must name where prompts and records go.
7. **Letting outside AI clients configure the system.** Dotmatics exposes configuration writes to external Claude or ChatGPT clients over MCP. In this LIMS, configuration such as Check Plans, Specifications and roles is QA-approved and versioned. It must never be reachable from an assistant.
8. **Features that are really roadmap.** LabVantage's agents are "in development… built bespoke for each customer". Thermo's OpenAI and NVIDIA deals name no lab product. Treat these as intent, not evidence of what a regulator has accepted.

## Not settled here

- Invisible, always-on patterns are ticket #40.
- Model prices, retention terms, prompt injection and version pinning are ticket #42.
- Empower 3.10 and waters_connect 4.5 release notes could not be read. They may contain AI features this survey missed.
