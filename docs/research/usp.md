## Short answer

USP–NF shapes the LIMS in fewer places than Part 11 or ISO/IEC 17025, but where it does, it sets numbers the software must compute against, not just records to keep.

- **What is binding.** The General Notices and chapters numbered below 1000 are mandatory where a monograph or the General Notices make them apply. Chapters 1000–1999 are informational "regardless of citation" (GN 3.10, current wording). FDA, ANAB and Customers still expect them to be followed, mostly through 21 CFR 211.160/211.194 and ISO/IEC 17025 7.2. Every check in the LIMS should carry its basis (USP mandatory, USP guidance, ISO 17025, SOP) so an auditor can tell which numbers are the lab's own choices.
- **USP sets almost no frequencies.** No daily balance check, no quarterly pipette check, no daily Karl Fischer titer: those are SOP choices, risk-based per `<41>` and GN 3.10. Frequencies must be editable data under change control, not code.
- **Rounding (GN 7.20).** Keep full precision. Round only the reportable value, to the number of decimal places in the limit, round-half-up, and only then compare. Limits are "not rounded". A result rounded before the comparison, or with banker's rounding, is wrong.
- **Storage classes (`<659>`).** Freezer −25 to −10 °C, refrigerator 2–8 °C, controlled room temperature 20–25 °C (MKT NMT 25 °C, excursions 15–30 °C), cool 8–15 °C. The −80 °C freezer is not a USP class; its limits come from the SOP. `<1079.2>` adds controlled cold temperature and the MKT method, and says MKT must never be used to excuse a system that is out of control. A PF 52(4) proposal would change CRT to 15–25 °C.
- **Balances (`<41>`, revised, official 1 Feb 2026).** Calibration over the operating range, with measurement uncertainty. Periodic sensitivity (accuracy) and repeatability checks, each against 0.10 %. Repeatability is judged against the balance's **smallest net weight**, which must not be below its **minimum weight**; a weighing below it is not "accurately weighed" (GN 8.20). `<1251>` adds a safety factor (typically 2) and a 0.05 % target for single properties.
- **Instruments (`<1058>`).** The official chapter is still the 2017 "Analytical Instrument Qualification" (DQ/IQ/OQ/PQ, Groups A/B/C). The "Analytical Instrument and System Qualification" rewrite (PF 51(2), 2025) is **not official**; re-publication is targeted for PF 52(6) or PF 53(1).
- **Chromatography (`<621>`, harmonized, official 1 Dec 2022).** "No sample analysis is acceptable unless the suitability of the system has been demonstrated": a failed or missing system suitability criterion blocks every Reportable Result from that Run. Adjustments to a compendial procedure are allowed only within the listed ranges, and only once. The System Sensitivity and Peak Symmetry parts were postponed to 1 Jun 2026; the final text was not verified.
- **Reference standards (`<11>`, GN 5.80).** A current USP lot has no expiry date; a lot that becomes "previous" gets a valid-use date and cannot be used after it. The lab must check lot status in the USP catalogue. The current FAQ says 100.0 % purity cannot be assumed without an assigned value.
- **Data (`<1010>`, `<1029>`).** An outlier test "cannot be the sole means" of removing a result (2012 text; current wording not verified). Excluded values stay in the record. `<1029>` asks for contemporaneous, attributable, correctable-without-obscuring entries, which the project's audit-trail design already gives.
- **Nitrosamines (`<1469>`, informational, official 1 Dec 2021).** Its four procedures are validated "in selected sartans" only. Limits come from the regulator's acceptable intake, not from USP; no NDSRI revision was found.
- **Elemental and GC.** Harmonized `<233>` is official 1 May 2026 (spike recovery 70–150 %, repeatability RSD NMT 20 %, SST drift NMT 20 %). `<467>` was revised to ICH Q3C(R9), official 1 Aug 2025.

**Source caveat.** usp.org and uspnf.com return HTTP 403 to automated clients, and the official USP–NF text sits behind a subscription (online.uspnf.com, 403 by every route; doi.usp.org shows only a first paragraph or a briefing). The researchers therefore read, in order of preference: USP's own public documents (FAQs, commentaries, notices, revision bulletins, harmonized-text PDFs), many through Internet Archive snapshots or a headless browser; older official USP texts; full-text USP printouts on third-party hosts, labelled with their edition; and, only where nothing primary was reachable, secondary sources, labelled as such. **No current official chapter text was read**, except the harmonized `<621>` (2022) and `<233>` (2026) texts and `<1220>` and `<1079.2>`. Every row names its source and says "not verified" where a number could not be checked. Before any default below is locked into code, the lab should confirm it against its own USP–NF subscription; the numbers most in need of that are flagged in [Open questions](#open-questions-for-the-owner).

**Current edition.** USP–NF has published six issues a year since July 2025 [USP-PUB]. On 2026-09-29, Issue 4 content is official (since 1 Aug 2026), Issue 5 becomes official on 1 Oct 2026 and Issue 6 on 1 Dec 2026.

## Design implications for the equipment ticket (#15)

Each item names the record fields and the gate. Numbers marked *secondary* or *not verified* are defaults to confirm against the official text before they are locked.

1. **One scheduled-check engine with a basis on every check.** Each check type carries its basis (USP mandatory, USP guidance, ISO/IEC 17025, SOP) and a frequency held as data. USP sets almost no frequencies (GN 3.10; `<41>` FAQ: "no requirement for a daily" check), so the lab's daily, weekly and quarterly intervals are SOP choices, edited under change control.
2. **Balances (`<41>`, `<1251>`, GN 8.20).** Record per balance: capacity, readability d, ranges, smallest net weight, current minimum weight (from the last repeatability test), safety factor (default 2), calibration certificate (as-found, as-left, measurement uncertainty, due date), and the check weights used (ID, class, MPE, certificate due date). Sensitivity check: one external weight between 5 % and 100 % of capacity, limit 0.10 % (`<41>`), with 0.05 % as the `<1251>` target. Repeatability: NLT 10 replicate weighings of one weight NMT 5 % of capacity and at least 100 mg nominal; pass when 2s / smallest net weight ≤ 0.10 %, with s floored at 0.41d. **Gates:** a failed or overdue calibration, sensitivity or repeatability check blocks every "accurately weighed" step on that balance; a net weight below the smallest net weight is blocked; a check weight whose MPE is too large or whose certificate has expired is rejected; relocation or service forces requalification (level, internal adjustment, performance checks, calibration). The built-in weight is logged but never replaces the external-weight check (not metrologically traceable, per the USP FAQ).
3. **Pipettes (`<31>`, GN 8.20).** Record per pipette: type (glass class A, or piston-operated with volume range), gravimetric results per volume tested (mean error, CV), water temperature, the balance used, due date. Tolerances are configurable (ISO 8655, or the revised `<31>` with piston-operated apparatus once official; neither verified here). The quarterly frequency is an SOP choice. **Gate:** an overdue or failed pipette cannot be used for an "accurately measured" step.
4. **pH meter (`<791>`).** Record per calibration: buffer lots and expiry, nominal and temperature-corrected values, temperature, slope %, offset mV, check-buffer reading, and temperature-probe calibration. Defaults *secondary* (Thermo Fisher summary of the 2014 text): at least two buffers bracketing the sample, slope 90–105 %, offset ±30 mV, check buffer ±0.05 pH. No USP source for a 95–105 % slope was found. **Gate:** no sample pH is recorded without a passing calibration for that day whose buffers bracket the sample value.
5. **Karl Fischer (`<921>`).** Record: titrant lot, water equivalence factor (mg/mL) with its replicate results, the standard used (water or sodium tartrate dihydrate) and its lot, date and time. How often USP wants the factor re-determined was **not verified**; "daily" appears only in non-USP sources. **Gate:** a water result must cite a factor inside the SOP validity window. Keep the raw inputs, since USP noticed an error in a %-water formula it had added (revision targeted 1 Dec 2025, not verified).
6. **DI water dispenser (`<643>`, `<645>`, `<1231>`, GN 8.230.30).** "Water" in a compendial procedure means Purified Water unless stated. 18.2 MΩ·cm is **not** a USP requirement for Purified Water; it applies only where a procedure asks for it (for example ICP-MS diluents). Log resistivity and TOC readings with time as fitness-for-use data. Methods declare the water grade they need, and a draw below that grade is blocked only for those methods. If the lab tests water to `<643>`, keep the system-suitability record (response efficiency 85–115 %); a failed SST flags every TOC result since the last pass for review. The dispenser's built-in monitor is labelled "monitor", not a `<643>` test, unless it is qualified to the chapter. `<645>`: store uncompensated conductivity, temperature and stage; cell constant within ±2 % of its certified value.
7. **Storage units: freezers, refrigerators, rooms and chambers (`<659>`, `<1079.2>`, GN 10.10).** Each unit, or each compartment of the −20 °C / 3–8 °C combination unit, gets a class: freezer (−25 to −10 °C), refrigerator (2–8 °C), cool (8–15 °C), CRT (20–25 °C), or a custom class with SOP limits (−80 °C; stability chambers at their ICH condition). A reading outside the limits creates an excursion with duration and peak. MKT can be computed (ΔH 83.144 kJ/mol; 24 h window for cold, 30 days for CRT), but it never closes an excursion on its own: a person records a disposition, and repeated excursions escalate to a Deviation (#16). **Gate:** an inventory item whose label storage class does not match its unit's class cannot be placed there; items in a unit with an open excursion are held until the disposition. CRT limits stay configurable because of the PF 52(4) proposal.
8. **Temperature and humidity devices (GN 6.80.30, `<1118>`).** Every logger, probe and thermometer has a calibration record traceable to NIST or equivalent, with a due date. An overdue device marks its readings "unverified" and flags the units it monitors.
9. **Instrument qualification (`<1058>`, `<621>`, `<736>`, `<730>`).** Record per instrument: group (A, B, C) by intended use, user requirements, DQ, IQ, OQ and PQ records with dates, approvals and documents, firmware and software versions, change-control link, requalification due date. For LC-MS/MS and GC-MS, a scheduled tune and mass-calibration check (reference compound, observed mass error, pass/fail); `<736>` (2014 commentary) keeps ±0.50 m/z for quantitative work. For ICP-MS, the manufacturer's tune and mass calibration (`<730>`, USP 37 text). **Gates:** an instrument without an approved OQ/PQ, with an overdue PQ, or with an overdue or failed tune check cannot start a GMP Run (`<621>`: "The various components of the equipment employed must be qualified"); a software or firmware change forces an impact assessment. Model the qualification stages as data so the proposed AISQ three-stage life cycle can replace the 4Qs without a schema change.

## Design implications for the other tickets

- **Results entry and review (#20).** Each Run holds a system suitability record: parameter, criterion, criterion source (method, monograph or `<621>` default), value, pass/fail. A failed or missing criterion blocks every Reportable Result from the Run and leads to an invalidation through a Deviation, never an edit (`<621>`). Adjustments to a compendial procedure are logged as {parameter, original, adjusted, allowed range, check computed}, with values outside the allowance, any detector-wavelength change and a second adjustment blocked. Store full precision and round only the Reportable Result, to the Specification's decimal places, round-half-up (GN 7.20). An outlier test cannot by itself exclude a Result; excluded values stay visible with their reason, and outlier tests are allowed only if the Method pre-specifies them (`<1010>`, FDA OOS guidance). A standard's drying or water correction is recorded before its value enters a calculation (`<11>`). Store SST and check-standard values as numbers so `<1220>` Stage 3 trends are possible later.
- **Specification and limits (#29).** Store each limit exactly as written, with its decimal places, never as a float that loses trailing zeros (GN 7.10). NMT maps to ≤, NLT to ≥, limits include their stated values. Nitrosamine limits cite the regulator's acceptable intake and the maximum daily dose, not `<1469>`. Elemental limits cite the PDE and its `<232>` or ICH Q3D version and the compliance option; residual-solvent limits cite class, PDE, Option 1 or 2 and the `<467>` version. A reporting threshold on each line; totals sum only peaks above it (`<621>`, `<1086>` proposal).
- **Inventory (#17).** USP Reference Standard lots record catalogue number, lot, status (Current, Previous, Withdrawn), valid-use date (empty while Current), last catalogue check (date, user), assigned value and basis, qualitative-only flag, label storage, pretreatment, date opened, location. Non-USP materials keep a separate expiry or retest date; never map "valid-use date" onto "expiry". **Gate:** a Run cannot cite a Previous lot past its valid-use date, a Withdrawn lot, a lot overdue for its catalogue check, or a lot with no assigned value used quantitatively (`<11>`, GN 5.80). CRMs for elemental work record NMI traceability (`<233>`). Buffers, KF titrant and check weights are inventory items with lots and expiry.
- **Document vault (#18).** A Method is flagged compendial, alternative to compendial (with its equivalence report, GN 6.30) or in-house. Method Adoption needs an approved report matching its basis: validation (`<1225>`, by category I–IV), verification (`<1226>`, per Lab and, for APIs, per supplier or route where the impurity profile may differ), or transfer or justified waiver (`<1224>`). A `<1469>` procedure applied outside sartans is an in-house method. Validation records should already hold ICH Q2(R2) items (reportable range, replication strategy), since the `<1225>` rewrite in PF 51(6) is pending.
- **Stability (#31).** `<1150>` was deleted in 2012; cite `<1079.2>` for MKT and ICH Q1A(R2) for conditions. Chambers are storage units with a custom class. `<1191>` (pharmacy dispensing) does not apply.
- **Deviation (#16).** Opened by: a failed SST where results are affected, a storage excursion not dispositioned or repeated, a failed TOC SST (all results since the last pass), and a Withdrawn RS lot (list the Results that cite it).
- **Training (#19).** `<1226>` and `<1224>` expect documented training before an Analyst runs a verified or transferred procedure; this matches the Training Record gate already decided.

## Open questions for the owner

1. **Confirm the flagged numbers against a USP–NF subscription.** The defaults most at risk are the `<791>` pH criteria (secondary only), the `<645>` stage limits (secondary), the `<31>` tolerances (not reached), the `<921>` titer frequency (not found), and the current wording of GN 7.20 and `<1010>`. Does the lab hold USP–NF online access that someone can check before #15 and #20 lock these in?
2. **Which test-weight rule for balances?** USP's own documents differ: the 2014 `<41>` and the 2026 commentary say the test weight's MPE or uncertainty must be NMT one-third of the test limit; another commentary response says 0.05 %. The recommendation is one-third of the limit, configurable, until the current text is read.
3. **Does the lab run compendial procedures at all?** If every nitrosamine method is in-house validated, `<621>` adjustment allowances never apply to them (changes go through revalidation), and `<1226>` verification applies only to monograph tests such as water, residual solvents or elemental impurities.
4. **What are the −80 °C freezer and stability-chamber limits?** USP defines no −80 °C class, so the SOP or the manufacturer's specification sets them.
5. **Balance check frequency.** USP no longer expects a daily check; the map lists daily verification of 5 balances. Keep daily as the lab's SOP choice, or move to a risk-based interval?
